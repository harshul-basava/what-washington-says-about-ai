What does Washington say about AI?

For every member of Congress and every newly elected member, we collected what they have said publicly about AI: floor speeches, hearings, posts on X, interviews and press releases. We checked every quote against its source. A language model then rated each person on five questions, citing only those quotes.

Method

  1. Who: all 539 current members (senators, representatives and delegates) from the unitedstates/congress-legislators project, plus winners of the November 2026 elections who aren't already in Congress, taken from called races only. The January 2027 view combines continuing members with members-elect. A senator stays unless their seat was up in 2026 and someone else won it; a member who won a 2026 race stays, in the chamber they won.
  2. Official records: AI-related quotes from the Congressional Record and hearings, from CeSIA's Politicians on AI dataset (CC BY 4.0). Only the quote text, date and source are used. CeSIA's own labels are not.
  3. X: each person's posts since January 2023 that match AI keywords (retweets excluded), from X's full-archive search. For the first 132 people we used X's web search instead, which found about 94% as many posts in a side-by-side check.
  4. Web: a language model (Claude Sonnet 5.5) with web search looked for every substantive public statement about AI and was told explicitly that finding nothing is fine. We fetched every source page and kept a quote only if it appears there with a fuzzy match of at least 90%. When a site blocks automated access, we checked against the page text the search engine had retrieved.
  5. Scoring: the model saw only the verified statements for one person (no names of other sources, no party labels and no prior scores) and scored five questions on a written 0–4 rubric, or answered "insufficient evidence". Only the member's own views count: quoting someone else's warning (for example an AI company CEO's) doesn't count unless the member clearly adopts it. We ran it twice with the statements shuffled. If the two runs differed by 2 or more on any question, the person was flagged for review; otherwise the two scores were averaged.
  6. Review: a model from a different family re-scored everyone blind and audited the highest scores on AGI and existential risk, a random sample, and every case where the two models disagreed.
  7. Committees: current full-committee rosters from the @unitedstates project.

Caveats. What people say in public is not the same as what they believe or how they vote. Scores are produced by language models and only spot-checked. Official records lag by months. X coverage depends on knowing the right accounts, and some members barely post. Members who rarely talk about AI will have little or no evidence. That doesn't mean they hold no views.
