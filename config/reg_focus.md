# Regulation focus areas: rubric

The `regulation` question asks *how much* a member wants rules on AI. This rubric asks *which* rules. Each focus
area below is one concrete policy. Decide whether the member's own public statements show that they **support** or
**oppose** that policy. Most members say nothing about most areas. Then the answer is "no stance".

## Focus areas

### Everyday harms

| ID | Policy | Support means the member wants... | Oppose means the member... |
|---|---|---|---|
| `kids_chatbots` | Limits on AI chatbots for minors | a ban on AI companion chatbots for minors, age verification for AI chatbots, or a legal duty for chatbot makers to protect minors (e.g. GUARD Act, CHAT Act) | says such limits go too far, or works against a named bill that sets them |
| `deepfakes` | Penalties for harmful deepfakes | criminal or civil penalties, or takedown duties, for non-consensual intimate deepfakes, AI child sexual abuse material, or AI impersonation and fraud; a legal right over one's own voice and likeness (e.g. TAKE IT DOWN Act, NO FAKES Act) | opposes such penalties or rights (e.g. on free-speech grounds) |
| `election_ads` | Ban deceptive AI in election ads | a ban on materially deceptive AI content about candidates, or a duty to disclose AI in political ads and robocalls | opposes rules on AI election content, e.g. calls them censorship |
| `labeling` | Required AI labels and watermarks | a legal duty to watermark or label AI-made content, or to tell people when they talk to a chatbot | opposes labeling or disclosure mandates |
| `dc_moratorium` | Data center moratorium | a pause, moratorium or ban on building new AI data centers, nationally or locally | explicitly rejects a pause on data center construction |
| `dc_ratepayers` | Data centers pay their own power costs | a rule that AI data centers, not households, pay for the power plants and grid upgrades they need, or limits on electricity rate increases caused by data centers | opposes such rules, or wants ratepayers to share these costs |
| `layoff_reporting` | Required reporting of AI layoffs | companies must report or give notice of layoffs or hiring changes caused by AI (e.g. AI-Related Job Impacts Clarity Act) | opposes such reporting |
| `bias_audits` | Bias audits for AI decisions | a duty to test or audit AI for discrimination, or a ban on discriminatory AI, when AI makes decisions about jobs, housing, credit, health care or policing | opposes such rules, or calls them "woke AI" rules to remove |
| `training_consent` | Consent and pay for AI training data | AI companies must get consent from, or pay, the owners of copyrighted work they train on, or must disclose the work they trained on | says training on copyrighted work should stay legal without consent (e.g. fair use) |
| `preemption` | Federal preemption of state AI laws | one federal AI standard that overrides state AI laws, or a moratorium on state AI laws | wants states to keep the right to make their own AI laws |
| `chips` | Stricter AI chip export controls | stronger controls on exports of AI chips or chip-making tools to China and other rivals, or a ban on selling advanced chips to them | wants looser controls or more advanced chip sales to China |

### Catastrophic and frontier risk

| ID | Policy | Support means the member wants... | Oppose means the member... |
|---|---|---|---|
| `testing_mandate` | Mandatory safety testing before release | a legal duty to test the most advanced AI models for dangerous abilities before release, by the government or independent auditors | opposes mandatory testing or audits (support only for voluntary testing or for funding a testing body does not count) |
| `transparency` | Required safety plans and incident reports | AI companies must publish safety plans, report serious incidents, or share information about their most powerful models with the government | opposes such duties |
| `whistleblowers` | AI whistleblower protections | legal protection for AI company workers who report risks (e.g. AI Whistleblower Protection Act) | opposes them |
| `liability` | Developer liability for AI harms | AI developers can be held liable for harms their systems cause; no Section 230 shield for AI | opposes new liability for AI developers |
| `misuse` | Safeguards against AI weapons help | rules to stop AI from helping people make biological, chemical, nuclear or cyber weapons (e.g. required safeguards, DNA synthesis screening, testing for these abilities) | opposes such rules |
| `nuclear` | Human control of nuclear launch | a human, not AI, must make any decision to launch nuclear weapons | opposes this |
| `autonomous_weapons` | Human control of lethal force | a human in the loop for lethal force, limits or bans on lethal autonomous weapons | opposes limits on autonomous weapons |
| `licensing` | Licensing or a new AI agency | a license or approval to build or release the most powerful AI, or a new federal agency to oversee AI | opposes licensing or a new AI agency |
| `intl_agreement` | International AI safety agreement | a treaty or formal agreement with other countries, including China, on AI safety or AI risk (e.g. limits on dangerous AI, shared red lines, verification) | opposes such agreements, e.g. as a threat to U.S. sovereignty or lead |
| `pause` | Pause on frontier AI | a pause, moratorium or slowdown on frontier AI development, compute limits, or agreements that pace AI progress | explicitly rejects a pause or slowdown |
| `rsi` | Ban on self-improving AI | limits or a ban on AI that improves itself (recursive self-improvement) or fully automated AI research | opposes such limits |
| `superintelligence` | Ban on superintelligence | a ban or prohibition on building superintelligence (AI that is smarter than humans at nearly everything), at least until it is proven safe | explicitly rejects such a ban |

## Stance levels

| Value | Test |
|---|---|
| `2` | **Explicit support.** The member names this kind of rule or a bill that does it and says it should happen, or says they wrote, sponsored, cosponsored or voted for it. |
| `1` | **Implied support.** The member clearly calls for action on this specific harm or risk and a rule is the plain meaning, but does not name the rule. Example: "Congress must stop chatbots from preying on our kids." |
| `-1` | **Implied opposition.** The member criticizes this kind of rule in general terms. Example: "Burdensome AI disclosure mandates will kill innovation." |
| `-2` | **Explicit opposition.** The member says this rule or a named bill that does it should not happen, or that they voted against it. |
| (omit) | **No stance.** The default. |

## Rules

- **Their own position only.** Use what the member says, writes, signs or sponsors. A joint letter or bill counts for each signer. Do not count what they report that others said, and do not count a question they ask in a hearing unless it shows their own position.
- **A harm is not a policy.** Saying that deepfakes, data center power bills or AI risks are bad, or funding research, is not support for a policy. Support needs a call for this specific policy, or support for a bill that makes it. A general call to "protect workers" or "protect kids" is not support for any one policy here.
- **Specific, not general.** "We need guardrails on AI" alone is not a stance on any area. Give a stance only for the areas that the statement is about.
- **Broad bills count for their parts.** Support for a bill counts for each area that the member says the bill covers.
- **Opposition must be real.** A general wish for "light-touch" rules is opposition only to the areas that the member names or plainly means. "We must beat China, so no regulation" is opposition to `licensing` and `testing_mandate` only if the member talks about such rules.
- **Pick the strongest consistent stance.** If a member's statements disagree, give the stance of their most recent clear statement.
- **Cite.** For each stance, cite 1 to 3 statement IDs that show it best, and give a one-sentence reason.
