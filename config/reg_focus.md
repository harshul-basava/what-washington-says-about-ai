# Regulation focus areas: rubric

The `regulation` question asks *how much* a member wants rules on AI. This rubric asks *which* rules. For each focus
area below, decide whether the member's own public statements show that they **support** or **oppose** new rules
in that area. Most members say nothing about most areas. Then the answer is "no stance".

## Focus areas

### Everyday harms

| ID | Area | Support means the member wants... | Oppose means the member... |
|---|---|---|---|
| `child_safety` | Child safety | rules that protect minors from AI: limits on AI chatbots or companions for kids, age checks, duty of care, bans on AI-made child sexual abuse material | says these rules go too far, or works against a named child-safety AI bill |
| `deepfakes` | Deepfakes and likeness | penalties or takedown duties for non-consensual intimate deepfakes, rights over one's own voice and likeness (e.g. TAKE IT DOWN Act, NO FAKES Act), rules against AI impersonation and fraud | opposes such rules (e.g. on free-speech grounds) |
| `elections` | Elections and misinformation | rules on AI in elections: disclosure or bans of deceptive AI in political ads, rules against AI-made disinformation | opposes rules on AI election content or AI "misinformation" rules (e.g. calls them censorship) |
| `labeling` | Labeling AI content | watermarks or provenance for AI content, a duty to tell people when content or a chatbot is AI | opposes labeling or disclosure mandates |
| `data_centers` | Data center rules | rules on AI data centers: protect ratepayers from power costs, limits on water use, local say over siting, data centers pay for their own power | wants fewer rules for data centers: faster permits, removing environmental review for them |
| `workers` | Worker protections | rules that protect workers from AI: notice or reporting of AI layoffs, limits on AI surveillance or AI firing decisions, a say for workers in AI use | opposes such rules |
| `civil_rights` | Bias and civil rights | rules against algorithmic discrimination (hiring, housing, credit, policing), bias audits or impact assessments | opposes such rules, or calls them "woke AI" rules to remove |
| `copyright` | Copyright and creators | consent, credit or payment for creative work used to train AI; disclosure of training data | says training on copyrighted work should stay free of new rules (e.g. fair use) |
| `state_laws` | Letting states make AI laws | states keep the right to make their own AI laws; opposes federal preemption or a moratorium on state AI laws | supports federal preemption of state AI laws or a moratorium on them |
| `chips` | Chip export controls | stronger controls on exports of AI chips or chip tools to China and other rivals | wants looser controls or more chip sales abroad |

### Catastrophic and frontier risk

| ID | Area | Support means the member wants... | Oppose means the member... |
|---|---|---|---|
| `testing` | Safety testing and audits | safety testing or evaluation of advanced AI models before release, independent or third-party auditors, a government body that tests models (e.g. AI Safety Institute, CAISI) | opposes mandatory testing or audits |
| `transparency` | Frontier transparency | AI companies must publish safety plans, report serious incidents, or share information about their most powerful models with the government | opposes such duties |
| `whistleblowers` | Whistleblower protections | legal protection for AI company workers who report risks (e.g. AI Whistleblower Protection Act) | opposes them |
| `liability` | Liability for AI harms | AI developers can be held liable for harms their systems cause; no Section 230 shield for AI | opposes new liability for AI developers |
| `misuse` | Bio, chemical and cyber misuse | rules to stop AI from helping people make biological, chemical, nuclear or cyber weapons (e.g. screening, safeguards, testing for these abilities) | opposes such rules |
| `nuclear` | Human control of nuclear weapons | a human, not AI, must make any decision to launch nuclear weapons | opposes this |
| `autonomous_weapons` | Limits on autonomous weapons | a human in the loop for lethal force, limits or bans on lethal autonomous weapons | opposes limits on autonomous weapons |
| `licensing` | Licensing and a new AI regulator | a license or approval to build or release the most powerful AI, or a new federal agency to oversee AI | opposes licensing or a new AI agency |
| `international` | International coordination | treaties, agreements or talks with other countries (including China) on AI safety and risk, global safety standards | opposes international AI agreements, e.g. as a threat to U.S. sovereignty or lead |
| `pause` | Pause or pacing agreements | a pause, moratorium or slowdown on frontier AI development, compute limits, or agreements that pace AI progress | explicitly rejects a pause or slowdown |
| `rsi` | Limits on self-improving AI | limits or a ban on AI that improves itself (recursive self-improvement) or fully automated AI research | opposes such limits |
| `superintelligence` | Ban on superintelligence | a ban or prohibition on building superintelligence (AI that is smarter than humans at nearly everything), at least until it is proven safe | explicitly rejects such a ban |

## Stance levels

| Value | Test |
|---|---|
| `2` | **Explicit support.** The member names this kind of rule or a bill that does it and says it should happen, or says they wrote, sponsored, cosponsored or voted for it. |
| `1` | **Implied support.** The member clearly calls for action on this specific harm or risk and a rule is the plain meaning, but does not name the rule. Example: "Congress must stop chatbots from preying on our kids." |
| `-1` | **Implied opposition.** The member criticizes this kind of rule in general terms. Example: "Burdensome AI disclosure mandates will kill innovation." |
| `-2` | **Explicit opposition.** The member says this rule or a named bill that does it should not happen, or that they voted against it. |
| (omit) | **No stance.** The default. |

## Rules

- **Their own position only.** Use what the member says, writes, signs or sponsors. A joint letter or bill counts for each signer. Do not count what they report that others said, and do not count a question they ask in a hearing unless it shows their own position.
- **A harm is not a rule.** Saying that deepfakes or AI risks are bad, or funding research, is not support for a rule. Support needs a call for a rule, a ban, a duty, a limit or a penalty, or support for a bill that does one of these. Funding the AI Safety Institute or CAISI counts as support for `testing` only when the member says it should test or evaluate models.
- **Specific, not general.** "We need guardrails on AI" alone is not a stance on any area. Give a stance only for the areas that the statement is about.
- **Broad bills count for their parts.** Support for a bill counts for each area that the member says the bill covers.
- **Opposition must be real.** A general wish for "light-touch" rules is opposition only to the areas that the member names or plainly means. "We must beat China, so no regulation" is opposition to `licensing` and `testing` only if the member talks about such rules.
- **Pick the strongest consistent stance.** If a member's statements disagree, give the stance of their most recent clear statement.
- **Cite.** For each stance, cite 1 to 3 statement IDs that show it best, and give a one-sentence reason.
